Guide A & B • Geographical Itinerary

San Lázaro Archipelago Route

  • San Lázaro Archipelago (Archipiélago de San Lázaro): Discovered on the Sunday of St. Lazarus (March 17, 1521). Positioned at 10° N latitude and 161° from the Tordesillas Line of Demarcation.
  • Samar (Zamal): Spotted March 16 as "high land", 300 leagues (1,666.8 km) from Mariana Islands (Islas de los Ladrones).
  • Homonhon (Humunu): Uninhabited landing spot on March 17. Named Aguada de los Buenos Indicios ("Watering Place of Good Signs") due to freshwater springs and gold traces.
  • Suluan (Zuloan): Inhabited island where the fleet made its first contact with local inhabitants.
  • Southwestern Landmarks: Leyte (Cenalo/Ceylon), Hinunangan City (Hiunanghan), Hibusong (Ibusson), and Municipality of San Juan (Abarien).

Guide B • Limasawa & Pre-Colonial Waypoints

Limasawa (Mazaua) to Cebu Passage

  • Limasawa Island (Mazaua): Spotted March 27 via night fires; anchored March 28. Coordinates recorded at 9° 40' N (9⅔° N) latitude, 162° from the Demarcation Line, located 25 leagues (138.9 km) from Homonhon.
  • Intervening Passage: Passage through Leyte, Bohol, Canigao (Canigan), Baybay City (coastal city mistaken as an island), and Himokilan Island (Gatighan - 111.12 km from Limasawa).
  • Wildlife Documented at Himokilan: Eagle-sized bats, large black birds with goose-sized eggs, doves, and parrots.
  • Camotes Islands: Polo, Pacijan (Ticobon), and Ponson (Pozon) where the fleet waited for Rajah Colambu's fleet before proceeding to Cebu.

Guide A & C • Historical Value

Pigafetta’s Log & 1982 Spring Rediscovery

Pigafetta provided precise geographical, botanical, and resource notes—recording gold traces, white coral, palm wine production, and freshwater springs. In 1982, modern researchers used Pigafetta's exact directional notes to relocate Homonhon's original freshwater springs (Aguada de los Buenos Indicios) lost for over 450 years.

Guide A • First Encounters

Suluan & Homonhon Contact

  • Inhabitants from Suluan met the expedition on Homonhon's shore with a welcoming demeanor.
  • Magellan presented red caps, mirrors, combs, bells, fabrics, and ivory trinkets.
  • Locals responded with Uraca (palm wine), bananas, and coconuts. Pigafetta documented detailed accounts of coconut harvesting and local spice growth.
  • Locals returned 4 days later with live poultry, oranges, and coconuts.

Guide B • Pre-Colonial Alliances

Sanduguan & Pre-Colonial Networks

Magellan established contact with Rajah Colambu of Limasawa and his brother Rajah Siaiu of Butuan/Calaghan through the Malay interpreter Enrique of Malacca. Magellan and Rajah Colambu sealed a blood compact (Sanduguan). Magellan declined a large bar of gold offered by Rajah Colambu to emphasize alliance over commercial greed, leading to the Easter Sunday Mass in 1521.

Guide C • Critical Analysis

Deconstructing Early Colonial Tactics

Spanish gift-giving and cannon demonstrations were utilized as diplomatic soft-power strategies. Meanwhile, local Rajahs used alliance building with foreign naval fleets to reinforce their regional standing and trade networks.
